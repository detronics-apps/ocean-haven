# People, story, mini-games and the ending

BlueHaven added these after the systems (phase 7-8). Plan their hooks in phase 1 so the systems
are built with them in mind.

## The story's shape
- The loop every feature serves: meet → wonder → investigate → understand → act → watch →
  connect.
- Not "humans are destroying it, go fix it", but "look at this incredible place; something is
  out of balance; let's find out why".
- The player discovers; the game doesn't lecture. Facts come after the player has seen it.
- No preaching; wonder carries the message. Solutions must be honest and thought through.
- Real-world reason to care, but never pledges or simplistic "good choices".

## People
- Every person is a way of understanding the world, with a **general job title** (Researcher,
  Lighthouse keeper, Diver, Fisher, Engineer, Pilot) so they stay useful everywhere.
- Per area: one **objective-giver** (asks the questions that become objectives) and one
  **hint-giver** (advice, stories, never objectives); hint-givers point round the world.
- **Objectives only come from people**, as questions: the person asks, a few seconds later the
  objective appears; done → "go back to X" → thanks and the next question.
- Everything they say is worked out from the game state, never from past talks, so old saves
  meet everyone where they are; questions the player has got past are skipped.
- Asked again by a stuck player, a hint-giver points at what's actually holding things back now,
  a different thing each time; plus news the player may have missed, low-resource tips (only
  while getting started), observations about the animals (each a few times a game).
- The player has a name the people use. The player's replies are 2 choices, never words put in
  their mouth.
- People stand where they make sense (on land; an office for someone whose work is at sea).
- Predictions: a person asks what the player thinks will happen, keeps the guess, and later says
  what happened. No score.

## Mini-games (ranger activities)
- Real work done with a person, understood in 10 seconds; the first (story) play does real work
  in the game (a research mission at once); replays are for fun and personal bests, with a small
  daily grant, never more for a better score.
- Each is a drawn, living scene, not a grid of buttons; all at the same level of detail.
- Levels get harder; hearts (3) and records; out of hearts never means "you failed".
- Fits a phone screen; buttons wrap.

## Care mini-game (rescue companions)
- One young animal at a time, per area, found once its conditions hold; named by the player;
  cared for by hand (drag food to its mouth, stroke it, plaster on the wound) for a set number
  of days; then released, tagged, and seen again around the world.
- Bars can drop (fed empties; health drops only while hungry; calm only rises by comforting and
  drops while distressed), never to death (health floor). It won't eat until calm. A hatchling
  starts healthy with no wounds. Bars update live while watched; they freeze once it's ready.

## Collecting and the journal
- Things enter the journal only once photographed; every kind must be photographable.
- Photo moments: a few distinct poses or places per kind (BlueHaven: max 5 everyday ones incl.
  baby and young, plus a gold bonus for a seasonal moment), not one per day; the latest photo
  per moment is kept.
- Pay for photos once per kind per day, and tell the player how to get paid (a tangled animal's
  first tap frees it; the photo is the second tap).

## Living world touches
- Babies born beside a parent, following it while they grow, then their own spot.
- Animals roam their whole area, not just their building; some travel between areas once both
  are healthy; travellers can carry things (seeds that sprout trees in a treeless area).
- Seasonal moments: nice-to-have events, never progress-dependent, mentioned a week ahead.
- A one-time snapshot of each area on first arrival, to look back at in the ending.
- Cross-area links (clean water here speeds recovery there) shown at the end.

## The ending
- When the last step is done, the final place opens: every area as it lies, the links the player
  made, each area's score and the whole world's.
- A question with answers from the player's own game ("What have you learned?"), each person's
  closing line, then the credits: black screen, slow rolling text (with a speed button), the
  prize (a poster to download).
- Play goes on afterwards; the credits can be replayed.
